# Cinematic realism contract

## 1. Optics

Choose lenses by shot purpose:

- wide environment: natural wide perspective with controlled edge distortion;
- medium performance: normal perspective with spatial depth;
- close portrait: mild telephoto compression and restrained depth of field;
- detail insert: macro/selective focus with a readable focal plane.

Describe perspective, focus behavior, and depth relationship. Do not force one focal length across the entire video.

## 2. Exposure and color

- Retain texture in bright windows, practical lights, skin highlights, and reflective surfaces.
- Preserve readable shadow detail; avoid crushed blacks.
- Protect skin tones from aggressive teal/orange or neon grading.
- Use restrained grain, halation, bloom, or lens flare only when motivated.
- Prefer a coherent palette with scene-specific variation over a single fixed color wash.

## 3. Surface realism

Use imperfections that belong to the scene:

- pores, fine facial texture, slight asymmetry, flyaway hairs;
- cloth weave, seams, folds, tension, and gravity;
- fingerprints, dust, scratches, water droplets, scuffs, and wear;
- atmospheric depth, haze density, weather interaction, and imperfect reflections.

Avoid waxy skin, flawless synthetic surfaces, excessive sharpening, and uniform glossy materials.

## 4. Motion physics

- Give camera moves acceleration and deceleration.
- Preserve coherent parallax among foreground, subject, and background.
- Match motion blur to movement and shutter impression.
- Maintain gravity, balance, foot contact, hair/cloth inertia, and object weight.
- Keep focus and exposure changes gradual unless motivated by a cut or practical event.
- Use one dominant camera behavior per shot.

## 5. Human performance

- Prefer a specific playable action over generic “emotional performance.”
- Use small eye shifts, breath, posture changes, hand tension, and reaction timing.
- Keep mouth neutral in non-vocal shots.
- For direct lip sync, simplify head rotation, occlusion, foreground crossing, and camera shake.
- In groups, define spatial blocking and avoid simultaneous complex actions for every person.

## 6. Anti-patterns

Replace these vague phrases:

- `cinematic, photorealistic, 8K, masterpiece`
- `epic camera movement`
- `perfect skin`
- `dramatic lighting` without source or direction
- `dynamic action` without blocking

With concrete choices:

- motivated key and rim sources;
- framing-appropriate perspective;
- highlight roll-off and retained shadow detail;
- tactile imperfections;
- one physically plausible camera path;
- one clearly staged primary action.

Common negative constraints:

```text
No plastic skin, over-sharpened HDR, synthetic CGI gloss, weightless movement, anatomy warping, identity morphing, unstable hands, duplicate people, floating objects, unrequested captions, logos, watermarks, UI, storyboard grids, or split panels.
```

Use only constraints relevant to the shot; excessively long negative lists can dilute the main action.
